# EC and Nutrient Strength

## What EC measures
EC (Electrical Conductivity) measures the total concentration of dissolved
nutrient salts in the solution, expressed in mS/cm (or ppm in some regions).
It acts as a proxy for nutrient strength — too low and plants are underfed,
too high and plants can suffer nutrient burn or osmotic stress that makes it
harder for roots to absorb water even when nutrients are abundant. Each crop
and growth stage has an ideal EC range that generally increases as the plant
matures and its nutrient demand grows.

## How to reduce EC
To reduce EC, dilute the reservoir by adding fresh pH-balanced water without
additional nutrients. Do this gradually — adding too much water at once can
shock the root system with a sudden osmotic change. Continuously monitor EC
after dilution and adjust in small increments until the value falls back
within the crop's ideal range. In automated systems, a freshwater dosing
pump can perform this adjustment incrementally and re-check after each dose.

## How to increase EC
If EC is too low, dose concentrated nutrient solution (commonly labeled "A"
and "B" for two-part formulas that must be mixed into water separately to
avoid precipitation) in small increments, stirring or circulating well
between additions, and re-measuring before adding more.

## EC and growth stage
Seedlings need a much lower EC than mature, fruiting plants — dosing a young
seedling at full-strength adult EC is a common beginner mistake that causes
root burn and stunted growth. Ramp EC up gradually as the plant moves from
seedling through vegetative growth and into flowering or fruiting.
