# Growth Stage Lifecycle

## The four stages
Hydroponic crops typically move through four stages: Seedling (germination
and early root development, low nutrient strength), Vegetative (rapid
leaf/stem growth, increasing EC), Flowering/Fruiting (reproductive
development, highest nutrient and light demand for fruiting crops), and
Harvest (final maturation, sometimes with slightly reduced EC or
temperature to improve quality). Each stage has different ideal pH, EC,
light, and temperature targets — using one fixed setting for the entire
grow cycle under-feeds young plants or over-feeds mature ones.

## Seedling stage
Young plants have small, fragile root systems and are easily stressed by
strong light, high EC, or temperature swings. This stage prioritizes gentle
conditions: lower light intensity, lower EC, and stable, moderate
temperature to establish healthy roots before pushing growth.

## Vegetative stage
The plant rapidly expands leaf area and root mass. Light intensity and
duration typically increase, and EC is ramped up gradually to match rising
nutrient demand. This is also when training techniques (like pinching herbs
or trellising vines) are most effective.

## Flowering / fruiting stage
Reproductive development begins. Fruiting crops usually need their highest
EC and light intensity here to support flower and fruit development, plus
attention to pollination for crops like tomato and cucumber. Leafy greens
like lettuce are generally harvested before reaching true flowering.

## Harvest stage
Final maturation before harvest. Some growers slightly adjust EC or
temperature in this window to improve flavor, texture, or shelf life — for
example, lowering temperature slightly before harvesting lettuce improves
crispness.
