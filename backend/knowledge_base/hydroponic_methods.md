# Hydroponic Methods

## Deep Water Culture (DWC)
Plant roots hang directly into an oxygenated nutrient solution reservoir,
suspended by net pots in a floating platform. DWC is simple to build and
maintain, gives fast growth from constant nutrient and oxygen access, and
has low mechanical complexity — making it an excellent beginner-friendly
choice, especially for leafy greens and herbs. Its main weakness is
dependence on a reliable air pump: roots can suffocate quickly during a
power outage.

## Nutrient Film Technique (NFT)
A thin, continuously flowing film of nutrient solution runs along sloped
channels over exposed plant roots. NFT is very water and nutrient efficient
since the solution recirculates, and gives good root oxygenation from the
thin film exposure. It scales well for commercial leafy green production but
is highly vulnerable to pump failure — roots can dry out within minutes if
flow stops — and is not ideal for large root systems or heavy fruiting crops.

## Ebb and Flow (Flood and Drain)
A grow tray is periodically flooded with nutrient solution, then drained
back to a reservoir. This method is flexible for many crop types and media
setups, gives a good balance of oxygen and nutrient delivery via the flood
cycles, and is somewhat tolerant of brief pump interruptions since the
growing media retains moisture between cycles.

## Drip System
Nutrient solution is delivered directly to the base of each plant through
drip emitters, either recovery (runoff is collected and reused) or
non-recovery. Drip systems give precise, individualized dosing per plant and
suit larger fruiting crops with high water demand, but emitters can clog and
need regular maintenance.

## Aeroponics
Roots are suspended in air inside a chamber and misted with nutrient
solution at short intervals for maximum oxygenation. This gives the fastest
potential growth and best water efficiency of any method, but is the most
technically demanding and the most sensitive to pump or nozzle failure —
roots can desiccate within minutes without misting.

## Vertical Farming
Stacked growing layers, often combined with NFT, DWC, or ebb-and-flow,
maximize yield per square meter of floor space using LED lighting. This
suits urban farms and fully controlled-environment agriculture, but comes
with high upfront investment and energy consumption, and is not practical
for tall or heavy fruiting crops.

## Choosing a hydroponic method
Choosing a method depends on crop type, budget, and technical experience.
DWC and Ebb and Flow are beginner-friendly and forgiving of minor mistakes.
NFT and Drip systems are more nutrient/water efficient but need reliable
pumps. Aeroponics offers the fastest growth and highest efficiency but is
the most technically demanding. Vertical Farming maximizes space for leafy
greens and herbs in controlled environments.
