# Light Intensity and Photoperiod

## Why light matters
Light drives photosynthesis and is measured by both duration (photoperiod,
hours/day) and intensity (commonly PPFD, µmol/m²/s). Leafy greens like
lettuce and basil generally need 14-16 hours of moderate-intensity light,
while fruiting crops like tomato and cucumber need higher intensity light
to support flowering and fruit development. Too little light slows growth
and causes leggy, weak stems; too much can cause leaf bleaching, heat
stress, or tip burn.

## Choosing a photoperiod
Photoperiod (how many hours of light per day) also signals developmental
cues to some plants — certain crops are sensitive to day length and can
shift toward flowering or bolting if the photoperiod changes unexpectedly.
For most controlled-environment hydroponic setups, a consistent daily
schedule is more important than mimicking natural seasonal changes.

## Light intensity by growth stage
Seedlings need lower light intensity than mature plants — strong light on
fragile young seedlings can cause bleaching and stress before the plant has
developed enough leaf area and root mass to use that energy productively.
Intensity is typically ramped up gradually through the vegetative stage and
peaks during flowering/fruiting for crops that produce fruit.

## Practical light management
Raising or lowering fixture height is the simplest way to manage intensity
without buying new equipment, since intensity falls off sharply with
distance from the light source. Keep a consistent distance across the
canopy to avoid uneven growth.
