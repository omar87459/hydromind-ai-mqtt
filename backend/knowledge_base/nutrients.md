# Nutrients and Deficiencies

## Macronutrients and micronutrients
Hydroponic nutrient solutions supply macronutrients (nitrogen, phosphorus,
potassium, calcium, magnesium, sulfur) in relatively large amounts, and
micronutrients (iron, manganese, zinc, boron, copper, molybdenum) in trace
amounts. A complete hydroponic fertilizer is formulated to supply all of
these in the right ratios — mixing your own from raw salts requires care to
avoid nutrient lockout or toxicity.

## Signs of nutrient deficiency
Common deficiency signs include yellowing between leaf veins on new growth
(often magnesium or iron, since these are less mobile within the plant),
overall pale yellowing of older leaves (nitrogen, which the plant moves from
old to new growth when scarce), purplish leaf undersides (phosphorus), and
browning leaf edges or tip burn (potassium or calcium). Deficiencies are
frequently caused by incorrect pH locking out nutrients rather than an
actual lack of nutrients in solution, so always check pH before assuming a
dosing problem.

## Root oxygenation and nutrient uptake
Roots need dissolved oxygen to actively respire and take up nutrients —
poorly oxygenated solution leads to weak roots, nutrient uptake problems,
and increased risk of root rot pathogens even when the nutrient formula
itself is correct. Air stones, air pumps, and the thin-film design of NFT
channels are all ways hydroponic systems keep oxygen levels high.

## Flushing between growth phases
Some growers flush the system with plain, pH-balanced water for a short
period before harvest or before switching nutrient formulas between growth
stages, to prevent salt buildup in the growing media and reset the root
zone before the next phase.
