# Root Health and Disease Prevention

## Root rot prevention
Root rot is usually caused by low dissolved oxygen combined with warm water
temperatures, creating ideal conditions for pathogens like Pythium. Prevent
it by keeping water temperature below 24°C, maintaining strong aeration,
keeping the reservoir dark to limit algae growth (light encourages both
algae and some root pathogens), and sanitizing equipment thoroughly between
grow cycles.

## Signs of root problems
Healthy hydroponic roots are typically white or pale and firm. Brown, slimy,
or foul-smelling roots are a strong sign of root rot or another pathogen
and warrant immediate action: increase aeration, check and correct water
temperature, and consider a beneficial-bacteria or hydrogen-peroxide
treatment appropriate for the system.

## Preventing algae growth
Algae competes with plants for nutrients and oxygen, and can clog drip
emitters and pump intakes. It thrives on light and warm, nutrient-rich
water — the most effective prevention is simply blocking light from
reaching the reservoir and any exposed nutrient solution (opaque reservoirs,
covered channels), rather than relying on treatment after it appears.

## Humidity and disease pressure
Humidity affects transpiration — the rate at which plants pull water and
nutrients up through their roots. Too low humidity causes excessive water
loss and leaf stress; too high humidity slows transpiration and increases
risk of fungal disease like powdery mildew or botrytis on leaves and stems.
Most hydroponic crops prefer 50-70% relative humidity, with fruiting crops
like cucumber tolerating higher levels.
