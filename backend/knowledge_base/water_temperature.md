# Water and Air Temperature

## Effects of high water temperature
Water temperatures above the crop's ideal range (typically above 24-26°C for
most crops) reduce dissolved oxygen levels in the nutrient solution, which
suffocates roots and increases the risk of root rot and pathogens like
Pythium. High water temperature also accelerates algae growth in exposed
reservoirs and can speed up nutrient degradation. Cooling fans, dedicated
water chillers, or simply shading and insulating the reservoir are common
mitigations.

## Effects of low water temperature
Water that is too cold slows nutrient uptake and root metabolic activity,
leading to stunted growth even when nutrient levels are correct. Most
hydroponic crops prefer water temperatures between 18-22°C. In cold
climates or seasons, a submersible aquarium-style heater can maintain
reservoir temperature within the ideal range.

## Air temperature and plant stress
Air temperature affects the rate of photosynthesis and transpiration.
Excessively high air temperature increases water loss and can cause wilting
even with adequate root-zone moisture; excessively low air temperature slows
growth and can trigger premature flowering (bolting) in leafy greens like
lettuce. Most hydroponic crops do best with air temperature 3-6°C warmer
than water temperature.

## Managing the day/night temperature swing
Many crops benefit from a modest day/night temperature difference (a few
degrees cooler at night), which mimics natural outdoor conditions and can
improve fruit and flower development in crops like tomato. Extreme swings,
however, stress the plant and should be avoided.
